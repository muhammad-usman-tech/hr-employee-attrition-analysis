# HR Employee Attrition Analysis

## Project Overview

This project explores employee attrition using Python and Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA).

The main objective is to understand employee-related patterns and investigate relationships between attrition and factors such as job satisfaction, environmental satisfaction, department, age, and monthly income.

This is a self-directed learning project created to practice Python-based data analysis and develop independent analytical thinking.

## Dataset

- **Dataset:** IBM HR Employee Attrition
- **Original Records:** 1,470 employees
- **Original Attributes:** 35 columns
- **Data Type:** Structured employee data

The dataset contains information about employee demographics, job roles, income, satisfaction levels, work experience, and attrition status.

## Key Questions Explored

- How does environmental satisfaction relate to employee attrition?
- How does attrition vary across departments?
- What does the employee age distribution look like?
- How does monthly income vary across departments?
- What relationships exist between years of experience, years at the company, and monthly income?
- How does attrition vary across marital status and education levels?
- Which departments and job roles account for the largest numbers of employees who left?

## Key Observations

The initial analysis produced the following observations:

- The Research & Development department had the largest number of employees in the dataset and the largest number of employees who left.
- Laboratory Technicians accounted for the largest number of departures among individual job roles in the grouped analysis.
- Age and monthly income showed a positive correlation.
- Years at the company and monthly income also showed a positive correlation.
- Attrition counts varied across marital status and education groups.

These observations are exploratory. Differences in employee counts do not necessarily indicate differences in attrition rates, and correlations do not establish causation.
